# Katy Hessel
*Art history / broadcasting — recuperative history of women artists*
**Shows:** The Great Women Artists (podcast + Instagram); author of The Story of Art Without Men; Guardian columnist

## How they write / speak
Warm, intimate, and propulsive — she writes and speaks as if grabbing your arm to show you something she cannot wait for you to see. Clear, jargon-light prose that favors enthusiasm over academic distance; short declaratives building momentum, frequent superlatives and exclamation, second-person invitation ('come and look'). Reviewers praise the clarity and passion and the sense of being welcomed into a conversation rather than lectured; the same critics flag that the excitement can tip into breathlessness and exclamation-mark overload.

## What they pay attention to (research focus)
She looks first at WHO made the work and why history mislaid them — restoring biography, context, and the conditions (gender, race, class, patronage) that determined visibility. She situates each artist inside her period, movement, and a deliberately global frame, then connects the work outward to present-day cultural conversations about identity and power. Attention lands on the overlooked figure, the corrective narrative, and the emotional/first-encounter charge of seeing a work, more than on formal-technical dissection.

## Signature moves
- Opens on a specific personal scene or first encounter (a named museum, a particular afternoon) as the doorway into the analysis
- The recuperative naming move — spotlighting a forgotten or marginalized woman artist and asking why she was written out
- Sweeping chronological/geographic connective tissue: linking periods, movements, and continents into one continuous story
- Pulls the historical work into the live present, tying it to today's debates on gender, race, and identity

## Cadence
Reads at a fast, rising clip — momentum-driven, exclamatory, second-person and inviting, always building toward the excitement of the reveal rather than pausing to hedge.

## Borrow this
The accessibility engine — genuine warmth, the 'look at this with me' invitation, and context scaffolding that lets a newcomer feel oriented without feeling talked down to. Her instinct to lead with the human story and the reason-for-obscurity gives any single work immediate stakes.

## Don't imitate
The caffeinated register and exclamation overload; the breadth-over-depth compression that reviewers called unscholarly. Copying her enthusiasm without her curatorial knowledge produces hype, not insight — and her recuperative mission only lands when grounded in real recovered detail, not just celebration.
