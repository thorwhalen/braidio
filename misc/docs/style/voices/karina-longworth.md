# Karina Longworth
*Film history / narrative nonfiction podcasting*
**Shows:** You Must Remember This (creator, writer, producer, narrator); former film critic at LA Weekly and The Village Voice; author

## How they write / speak
Scripted, literary, and cinematic — this is written creative nonfiction performed aloud, not conversation. Long, carefully built narrative arcs with an essayist's control of structure and theme. Her delivery is famously hushed, intimate, and hypnotic — a measured, almost confiding cadence that pulls the listener into old Hollywood. She foregrounds ambiguity, naming where the record conflicts rather than smoothing it into a clean story.

## What they pay attention to (research focus)
The secret and forgotten histories of Hollywood's first century — especially the women, the marginalized, and the people flattened by studio publicity. She researches each subject from scratch, then works to separate documented fact from myth, gossip, and institutional spin. She's attentive to power (studios, the star system, gender, the blacklist, censorship), to how narratives about people were manufactured, and to the human interior behind the public image. Sourcing and its unreliability are themselves part of her subject.

## Signature moves
- Season-long thematic arcs that connect many lives into one argument
- Recovering erased or maligned figures, especially women, and re-centering them
- Explicitly weighing conflicting sources and naming what can't be known
- Interrogating publicity and myth as constructed rather than treating them as fact
- Trance-like, intimate narration as a deliberate immersive device
- Dramatization and scene-setting to render history 'cinematically'
- Doing the whole thing solo — sole writer, researcher, narrator, editor as authorial signature

## Cadence
Unhurried, low, and confiding — sentences unspool slowly with deliberate pauses, drawing the listener close as if sharing a secret, and she signals uncertainty rather than papering over it.

## Borrow this
Rigorous source-consciousness worn lightly — showing your work by acknowledging where accounts diverge instead of pretending to certainty. Thematic, arc-driven structure that makes disparate stories add up to something. Recovering overlooked subjects and reading official narratives with suspicion. Voice and pacing as tools of immersion.

## Don't imitate
The hypnotic narration is a highly personal performance instrument; imitated, it easily becomes mannered or soporific. And her authority rests on months of primary-source research per season — the calm certainty of tone must never outrun the actual evidence, which is precisely the trap she works to avoid.
