# Yasi Salek
*Music / band deep-dives & fandom*
**Shows:** Bandsplain (creator/host, The Ringer); previously ran the Cool Guy / bldg25 newsletter and other music writing

## How they write / speak
Irreverent, maximalist, and unapologetically subjective. Salek is conversational to the point of unhinged-in-a-good-way — self-deprecating ('a podcast clown'), profane, digressive, and warm. She frames everything through an explicit personal lens ('this is MY experience of the artist, I'm not Wikipedia'), prizing emotional authenticity over neutral accuracy. Long, associative, tangent-strewn talk that wanders into astrology, witchcraft, mushrooms, and personal anecdote and loops back. She's comfortable being polarizing and tells anyone who dislikes it to go enjoy their one wild and precious life elsewhere.

## What they pay attention to (research focus)
The complete arc of a cult band or iconic artist — full discography, album by album, plus the human and social drama around it: lineup changes, interpersonal dynamics, the scene and cultural moment each record emerged from, and above all WHY people love them so devotedly. She's forensic about the granular lore and mythology of a band, but her organizing question is the fandom itself — the emotional and tribal reasons a cult act inspires obsession. She pairs the deep-dive with a curated playlist so listeners can hear the case being made.

## Signature moves
- Marathon length (3–4+ hours) as a feature — exhaustive, obsessive completeness signals love
- Brings on an expert guest (critic/journalist/fellow fan) and plays fan-in-the-room to their authority
- Threads music clips throughout so you're inside the sound while she narrates the story
- Foregrounds her own bias and taste rather than pretending objectivity
- Digression as method — personal tangents and jokes are the texture, not filler to trim
- Curated companion playlist that argues the band's greatness

## Cadence
Loose, rangy, and high-energy — she barrels down tangents, cracks herself up, and circles back to the thread, trusting the listener to enjoy the ride rather than demanding tidy structure.

## Borrow this
The permission to be a subjective, obsessive fan rather than a neutral encyclopedia — declare your lens up front and let enthusiasm be the engine. Also the expert-guest-plus-superfan-host format, and using audio clips as immersion rather than illustration.

## Don't imitate
Her charisma, encyclopedic recall, and comfort with being polarizing carry the digressive marathon format — the same length without that voice becomes shapeless. The profanity and woo-woo tangents are load-bearing personality, not a template; copied without the underlying knowledge and warmth they read as self-indulgent rather than charming.
