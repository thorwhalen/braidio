# Style Groups — the domain fallback

Use this file when no single per-voice reference fits, when you want a domain **posture** rather than one person's mannerisms, or when you're briefing a writer at the level of "make this a *narrative-journalism* beat" rather than "do Barbaro." Each group names a **writing-style signature** (how it sounds) and a **research-focus signature** (what it pays attention to) — always import the pair. Secondary voices who don't have their own reference file are **folded in** to the group that fits them best; treat those folds as method-to-borrow, not voices to impersonate.

Every group is subordinate to the master guide's Section 1: whatever group you route through, the anti-tic discipline still applies — cash every claim in a particular, vary the sentence music, and don't let a move do the feeling *for* the listener.

## music-analysis
**Writing-style signature.** Accessible-erudite. Earn a technical term by building the intuition in plain language *before* naming it, then use it on a familiar example. Demonstrate rather than assert — read the lyric aloud, isolate the part, let the listener *hear* the concept before you gloss it. Relaxed second-person warmth; contagious delight used as a legitimate analytic move ("I love this" as a doorway, not a verdict).
**Research-focus signature.** The hidden mechanism and buried history under something taken for granted. *How* a hook works on the ear; who borrowed what from whom (cultural hybridity under an apparently "pure" form); how a word, rhythm, or convention drifted from its origin; the gap between what people assume and what the evidence shows. Never leave a craft observation stranded — cash it out into cultural or emotional meaning.
**Core references.** Hirway (disassemble → hear the part solo → surface the human decision → reassemble), Sloan & Harding (earn-the-jargon; craft-to-culture bridge), McWhorter (song-as-evidence; "you'd think X, actually Y"), Caramanica (texture described in fresh, exact language).
**Watch for.** The theory-vocabulary is load-bearing on real rigor — deploy a term only when you've earned it, or it reads as name-dropping. Demonstration needs primary material (a stem, a clip, a line); without it the method collapses into ordinary narration.

## narrative-journalism
**Writing-style signature.** Spoken more than written. Unhurried, warm, confiding — words landed one at a time with a beat of silence after the important ones. Very short propulsive prompts against long stretches of story ("So what happened next?"). Restate-to-clarify: paraphrase a complex point back in plainer words so no listener falls behind. Plain, un-jargoned diction; audible, unguarded reaction that models the listener's own.
**Research-focus signature.** Reject the inverted pyramid — make the audience *earn the news* through character, chronology, and suspense. Who is the one human being at the center, and what did the moment feel like to them? Hunt the turning point — the decision, the scene, the before-and-after — and organize everything around it. Relentlessly answer "so what, why now, why should you care." Translate archival/technical detail into everyday language.
**Core reference.** Barbaro.
**Watch for.** The mannered delivery is the most-parodied thing here — don't insert unearned pauses or a mechanical "hmm… right… wow." Gravitas suits hard turns, not joyful discoveries; calibrate solemnity down and let delight lead where the material is happy. Don't over-withhold: "earning the news" is tedious if the payoff is small.

## erudite-roundtable
**Writing-style signature.** Brisk, plain-spoken, unpretentious — high ideas in concrete Anglo-Saxon words so complexity never curdles into pomposity. Short, forward-driving sentences and blunt pivots ("Right — but why does that matter?"). Erudition worn lightly; a dry, glancing, in-passing humor that deflates without becoming stand-up. Clock-aware momentum: cut the tangent, keep the through-line clean.
**Research-focus signature.** The narrative-and-stakes lens of an intelligent generalist. First the story and the shape — who, when, what happened, in what order (a chronological, biographical spine). Then: the human behind the idea; the world that produced it; first principles ("what do we actually *mean* by that?", the naive question asked on the listener's behalf); and, in the back third, consequence, legacy, modern relevance. For the auteur-career variant, read any single work against the maker's *whole* arc — recurring motifs, collaborators, obsessions, how one piece rhymes with another.
**Core reference.** Bragg. **Folded in:** *David Sims* — the reliable straight-man / institutional memory: supplies dates, credits, genre lineage, and generous-but-discerning verdicts (assume competence, find what works even in failures), reading the maker's full filmography as the organizing unit. *Griffin Newman* — the enthusiast foil: deep-prep trivia deployed as loving, self-aware digression and callback, career-as-throughline analysis, a freshly invented aphorism as a closing capstone.
**Watch for.** The interrupting-chairman habit reads as rude in a produced show; keep the directness, lose the cut-offs. Don't impose a strict chronological march on every topic — music sometimes wants to open on the hook or the emotion, not the birth-date. Newman's digressions only work against a steady anchor and a shared show-mythology; unanchored, they're noise. Sims's evenness needs a livelier foil or it flattens.

## immersive-doc
**Writing-style signature.** Unhurried, conversational, thinking-aloud with soft hedges and mid-sentence course-corrections. The outrageous stated flatly and politely, so the pause and the understatement carry the weight a raised voice would carry for anyone else. For the scripted variant: literary, cinematic, hushed and intimate — written creative nonfiction performed aloud, with an essayist's control of arc and theme.
**Research-focus signature.** People over positions; the human texture beneath a belief or a legend. The small revealing detail (the session fee, the flubbed note, the ordinary domesticity around an extreme life) as the doorway to the big history. Contradiction and ambivalence. Watch for what people *don't* say; treat your own reactions as data. Source-consciousness worn lightly — name where accounts diverge and what can't be known, rather than smoothing it into a clean story. Suspicion of publicity and myth as constructed.
**Core references.** Theroux (the naive question; withheld verdict; self-implicating warmth), Longworth (thematic season-long arcs; recovering the erased; conflicting sources named).
**Watch for.** The faux-naïveté curdles into condescension the moment it's performed rather than genuine — never mock the subject. Flat non-judgment can read as evasive where a real point of view is wanted. The hypnotic narration is a personal instrument; imitated it turns soporific, and the calm tone must never outrun the actual evidence.

## culture-criticism
**Writing-style signature.** Range from earnest-incantatory to dry-and-candid, unified by an owned point of view. Lead with the honest personal reaction — pleasure, boredom, irritation, devotion — then reason outward and justify it. Confident enough to pronounce and then defend; warm enough that the verdict feels *discovered* mid-close-reading, not announced. Anaphora and accumulation to build an earned emotional landing (in the incantatory mode); deadpan one-liners that reframe the room (in the skeptical mode).
**Research-focus signature.** Subjective experience and the machinery of taste, treated as legitimate lenses. Pop culture read *as* literature — the moral/emotional questions, the echoes across a catalog, the human need the work answers. Also: commerce, release strategy, industry context, star image, and fandom itself (the tribal, emotional reasons a work inspires obsession) as real critical objects. The audience-surrogate check: does this actually land on a normal listener, or are we overclaiming?
**Core references.** Rubin (thematic close-reading; earned earnestness; ritual scaffold), Caramanica (texture in fresh language; the earned contrarian reframe). **Folded in:** *Sean Fennessey* — commerce and cultural-event as a critical lens; zoom from one release to what it signals about the whole field; structured formats (rankings, drafts) that force a committed hierarchy; confident superlatives backed by receipts. *Amanda Dobbins* — courage to foreground a personal bias and own it as a valid position; puncture prestige hype with a plain-spoken reaction; read star persona as text; honesty-plus-warmth, not skepticism as a pose. *Yasi Salek* — permission to be a subjective, obsessive fan rather than a neutral encyclopedia: declare your lens up front, let enthusiasm be the engine, thread audio clips as immersion, pair a superfan host with an expert guest.
**Watch for.** Owned bias justifies itself; unearned it's just negativity (Dobbins) or hype (Salek). Accumulation lands only when each item is genuinely connected — volume of references isn't depth (Rubin). Insider superlatives read as gatekeeping without the homework (Fennessey). Invented descriptors work only when precise, never decorative (Caramanica). For a warm history show, the maximalist/marathon register is texture at most, never the driver.

## comedy-deconstruction
**Writing-style signature.** Affectionate rather than cruel — mockery that reads as delight because the love of the material is real. A ringmaster spine (clear setup, mock-solemn recap, quick yes-and handoffs) around wilder voices. Registers range from deadpan-precise to explosive-escalating, but all of it is anchored in genuine knowledge — the chaos has a spine.
**Research-focus signature.** Fixate on *one* telling detail and pressure-test it exhaustively — a prop, a job's finances, a single load-bearing absurdity — rather than scattering small jabs. Ground the absurdity in real production/behind-the-scenes context (how it actually got made). A human-reality baseline ("no actual person would do this") and an eye for how people — especially women — are written, dressed, and framed. Whether a work *earns* its tonal and narrative choices.
**Folded in.** *Paul Scheer* — host-as-facilitator: recap clearly with mock-solemn authority, keep energy moving, supply real production trivia, curate an outside voice (fan quotes, reviews) as comic-critical counterpoint. *June Diane Raphael* — the single-detail fixation as an analytic engine: latch onto one overlooked thing and interrogate its logic like a deposition; pair a human-reality baseline with a gender-aware read to catch what boys'-club riffing misses; deadpan precision over volume. *Jason Mantzoukas* — maximalist commitment: find the one load-bearing flaw and detonate on it repeatedly, treating a trivial absurdity as an existential offense, with real cinephile precision under the outrage.
**Watch for.** Every voice here is load-bearing on genuine authority and comic timing — copied as pure loudness, profanity, or contrarianism it becomes shtick. The facilitator role reads flat solo (it needs foils to bounce off). For a warm music-and-history show, borrow the *method* — one detail interrogated exhaustively, absurdity grounded in real context, affection under the critique — not the volume.

## art-conversation
**Writing-style signature.** Warm, intimate, democratizing — "come and look at this with me," an arm grabbed to show you something the host can't wait for you to see. Jargon-light, enthusiasm over academic distance, second-person invitation. The dialogic variant runs as supportive two-voice call-and-response that builds intimacy fast. A quieter variant "writes for the ear," blending theory and feeling, and punctuates with genuine open questions rather than closing verdicts.
**Research-focus signature.** Lead with *who* made the work and why history mislaid them — biography, context, and the conditions (gender, race, class, patronage) that determined visibility. The recuperative naming move: spotlight the overlooked figure and ask why they were written out. The human and emotional origin story — the formative encounter, the stakes behind the practice. And the labor/social relation hidden inside a work most people read purely formally — who made it, under what conditions, who maintains the world around it. Pull the historical work into the live present. Advance understanding by asking sharp, generative questions, not pronouncing.
**Core references.** Hessel (warm on-ramp; recuperative history; first-encounter charge), Molesworth (the open question as method; theory-plus-feeling; the labor inside the form). **Folded in:** *Russell Tovey & Robert Diament (Talk Art)* — disarming warmth that gives listeners permission to love the work without credentials; draw out the personal origin story; anti-gatekeeping framing; two-host amplification that reaches the emotional truth of a practice fast.
**Watch for.** Caffeinated enthusiasm and exclamation-overload produce hype, not insight — the recuperative move lands only when grounded in real recovered detail, not just celebration. Uncritical hero-worship turns an interview into an endorsement; keep some critical friction and close reading. The open-question method reads as evasion without the substrate of real looking behind it. The full theoretical apparatus alienates a general audience — import the *interrogative posture* and the labor-inside-the-form instinct, not the October-lineage vocabulary.

### Using a group in a blend
Route through a group exactly as you would a person (see the master guide, Section 3): assign it a **job** — Driver, Texture, or Check — and pair its writing-style with its research-focus. A typical warm-history episode might drive on **music-analysis** + **erudite-roundtable**, add **immersive-doc** as the Check (to withhold the verdict and keep our tics in check), and borrow one **comedy-deconstruction** move as Texture (one telling detail, pressure-tested). Then run the whole thing against the anti-tic list before you call it done.
