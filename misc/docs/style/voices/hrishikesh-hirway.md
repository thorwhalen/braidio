# Hrishikesh Hirway
*Music / songwriting-craft podcasting*
**Shows:** Song Exploder (creator/host); also The West Wing Weekly, Home Cooking, Partners, TED speaker

## How they write / speak
Quiet, spare, and unadorned. Hirway's signature is subtraction: on Song Exploder he edits his own questions out entirely, so the finished piece reads as a single uninterrupted first-person narration by the artist, stitched from interview audio and isolated song stems. When he does speak (intros, TED talk, guest hosting) his register is warm, low-key, precise, and emotionally sincere without being sentimental. Short declarative sentences, a musician's ear for pacing and silence, and a deliberate refusal to perform cleverness. The craft is invisible by design.

## What they pay attention to (research focus)
The creative process at the granular level, never the biography or the lifestyle. He isolates individual tracks/stems and asks origin questions about specific sounds: what is that, where did it come from, how did you make it, why did you keep it. He hunts for the hidden or accidental detail — the buried vocal, the demo mistake that stayed, the instrument that shouldn't work — and for the decision-point where a song could have gone another way. He treats a song as an assemblable/disassemblable object and wants the human intention behind each part.

## Signature moves
- Removes his own voice/questions from the final edit so the artist narrates uninterrupted
- Builds the episode from isolated multitrack stems, letting the listener HEAR each element solo before it's discussed
- Deep pre-listening to find 'the fun, hidden sounds' worth asking about
- Ends by playing the finished song in full, now that you understand how it was built
- Uses listening and silence as an interview tool — lets guests fill the space and reveal themselves

## Cadence
Calm, measured, and intimate — he leaves room to breathe between ideas and lets a musical fragment or a pause carry weight rather than rushing to fill it. The rhythm mirrors a producer listening back to a mix.

## Borrow this
The disassemble-then-reassemble structure: isolate a component, let the audience perceive it alone, surface the human decision behind it, then restore the whole so the listener re-hears the finished work with new ears. Also: privileging process over biography, and the discipline of getting out of the way.

## Don't imitate
The near-total self-erasure only works with genuine access to the creator and the raw stems/source material; without primary-source input it collapses into ordinary narration. His restraint reads as authentic, not a formula — copying the 'quiet' affect without the deep preparation behind it feels hollow.
